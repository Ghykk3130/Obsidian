## (a)
$$\begin{align}
E_{x} & = E_{0} \cos(kz-\omega t) \\
E_{y} & = E_{0}\cos(kz-\omega t+\delta)
\end{align}$$
At $z=0$, we have:
$$\begin{align}
 & E_{x}= E_{0}\cos \omega t \\
 & E_{y}=E_{0}\cos(\omega t-\delta)= \cos \omega t\cos\delta+\sin \omega t\sin\delta
\end{align}$$
Then:
$$\begin{align}
\sin \omega t= \frac{E_{y}-E_{0}\cos \omega t\cos\delta}{E_{0}\sin\delta}= \frac{E_{y}-E_{x}\cos\delta} {E_{0}\sin\delta}
\end{align}$$
Then: 
$$\begin{align}
 & \sin ^{2}\omega t+\cos ^{2}\omega t=1 \\
\implies & \left(  \frac{E_{y}-E_{x}\cos\delta}{E_{0}\sin\delta} \right)^{2}+ \left(  \frac{E_{x}}{E_{0}} \right)^{2}=1 \\
\implies & E_{x}^{2}+ E_{y}^{2}-2\cos\delta E_{x}E_{y}= E_{0}^{2}\sin ^{2}\delta \\
\implies & \begin{pmatrix}
E_{x} & E_{y} 
\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}
 1 & -\cos\delta \\
-\cos\delta & 1
\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}
E_{x} \\
E_{y}
\end{pmatrix}=E_{0}^{2}\sin ^{2}\delta
\end{align}$$
The eigen values are $\lambda=1\pm \cos\delta$, then the quadratic form is positive definite. Then it is an ellipse.

## (d)

We have:
$$\begin{align}
E^{2} & = E_{0}^{2}\cos ^{2}(kz-\omega t)+E_{0}^{2}\cos ^{2}(kz-\omega t+\delta)
\end{align}$$
Then we take the average:
$$\begin{align}
\langle E^{2}\rangle & = E_{0}^{2}\langle \cos ^{2}(kz-\omega t)\rangle+ E_{0}^{2}\langle \cos ^{2}(kz-\omega t+\delta)\rangle \\
 & = \frac{1}{2}E_{0}^{2}+ \frac{1}{2}E_{0}^{2} \\
 & = E_{0}^{2}
\end{align}$$
The result does not depend on $\delta$. Because the two electric fields are mutually orthogonal, the scalar interference cross-term vanishes identically. Consequently, the total intensity is simply the sum of the independent intensities carried by the $x$- and $y$-polarized modes. The relative phase $\delta$ governs only the relative timing between the orthogonal oscillations—determining the trajectory and eccentricity traced by the electric-field vector in the transverse plane —without redistributing or modifying the net electromagnetic energy flux.

For traveling waves, the waveform is moving in a certain direction. However, for standing waves, the waveform is not moving. All the nodes are fixed in space. For standing waves, we can decouple the space and time dependence. So that the spatial part $\cos(kx)$ pins a time-independent local amplitude envelope across space, while the temporal part $\cos(\omega t)$ drives all points to oscillate synchronously in time.

This week, Yiqian took the Hall data of the sample he has been working on. Previously, Yiqian only had the Nernst data, with the low temperature data showing the apparent topological signal. In order to confirm the nature of the signal, Yiqian did another PPMS run with the resistivity channel. However, since the resistivity of the sample is extremely small, the true signal is buried in noise for the PPMS default resistivity option, and external lock-in amplifier channel. In order to tackle down this issue, Yiqian switched to the resistance bridge, and obtained the low-noise data. The Hall data also showed apparent topological signals under low temperatures. However, there are still three problems. The direction of the field is different from the thin-film setup where people observed skyrmions with LTEM. The low-field feature may be coming from the two-channel anomalous hall effect mixing. A correct topological signal can not be extracted, due to the curvature difference between the magnetization data and the resistivity data. In order to explore the first possibility, Yiqian read papers about the structures other skyrmions that could produce topological signals. Then he concluded that real topological signal is possible because chiral structure is very likely to form in the material. For the second possibility, he also tried two-band effect fitting. But no consistent fitting could be obtained. For the third problem, Yiqian first attributed the reason to sample misalignment, and rescaled the data by a constant factor. However, the spike around the magnetization saturation is not smoothed out. Then his PI proposed that this might be due to that the demagnetization factor is not exactly 1 in the sample. Currently, he is working on doing a first-order correction to the field combing the demagnetization field.
